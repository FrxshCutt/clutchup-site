The full walkthrough

A practice drive, end to end.

From mounting the phone to reading the debrief — here is exactly what ClutchUp does, what it looks at, and what you stay in control of.

The drive flow

Everything is set up before you move. You never touch the app while driving.

1

Set up, stationary

Mount the phone, pick Free Drive or a Guided Route, and choose your analysis mode. All of it happens before you set off.

2

Checks

If you've enabled a camera mode, ClutchUp runs a quick road check — and, in face mode, a face check — so you know the framing is right.

3

Drive

Start recording and drive normally. Sensors, GPS and any enabled cameras run in the background while you concentrate on the road.

4

Debrief

End the drive and the analysis runs. Your examiner-style report is ready to read while it's all still fresh.

Two ways to drive

Structure when you want it, freedom when you don't.

Free Drive

Drive wherever you were going anyway — the shops, a lesson route, a motorway-free practice loop. ClutchUp records the whole thing and works out the junctions and manoeuvres from the map data as you go, so your debrief still knows where the tricky bits were.

Guided Route

Follow a planned learner-safe route with the manoeuvres laid out in advance. Because ClutchUp knows what's coming, it can open its mirror-check window at exactly the right moment on the approach to each planned turn.

Analysis modes

You decide how much
it analyses.

You pick a mode before every single drive, and camera use is entirely optional. Your choice is shown during the drive and stated on your debrief, so if a section is missing you always know why.

Telemetry only Motion sensors and GPS. No camera is opened at all. You still get a full debrief on braking, speed, cornering and smoothness.
Telemetry + road view Adds the rear camera as a road-facing view. Still images are captured only at detected driving events — there is no continuous video recording.
Telemetry + road + face view Adds front-camera mirror-check detection on the approach to turns and junctions, analysed entirely on your phone.

The road check and the face check

Thirty seconds of setup that stops you finishing a drive and finding the camera was pointing at the ceiling.

Road check

Before a camera drive, ClutchUp takes a single still through the rear camera to confirm the phone is actually mounted facing the road. If the framing is wrong it tells you how to adjust the mount before you start.

Face check

In face-view mode only, the app takes a few front-camera snapshots to confirm the camera can actually see you, and tells you how to adjust if not. They're analysed in memory and discarded immediately — neither the images nor the result are stored or uploaded. If it can't see you, you can still drive in a lower mode.

Mirror checks

When it isn't sure,
you're the judge.

As you approach a turn, the app opens a short window — around twenty seconds at most, and capped per drive — and measures head movement on-device to see whether you made a real mirror check.

  • Detected a check, missed it, or unsure — each turn gets an honest outcome.
  • Where the result is uncertain, you review a short clip of that moment yourself and confirm what actually happened.
  • That clip stays in private storage on your phone. It's never uploaded, never sent to any AI service, never added to your photo library, and never visible to a connected instructor.
  • It's deleted as soon as you've reviewed it, or automatically within 48 hours — whichever comes first.
  • Approach a junction but drive straight on? The window is discarded entirely — no result kept, frames deleted immediately.
Detected Clear head movement toward the mirror on the approach. Logged as a check and counted toward your observation score.
Unsure — over to you Borderline movement. You watch the six-second clip on your phone and tell the app whether you checked. Your answer is what counts.
Not detected No mirror movement found on the approach. It appears in your debrief as something to work on next drive.

What the debrief shows

Written the way an examiner would talk you through the drive you just did.

An overall verdict

A headline read on the drive, with faults grouped the way a test report groups them — driver faults, serious, dangerous.

Fault breakdown

Braking, approach speed, cornering, positioning and observation — each with the moments in your drive that produced the mark.

What to fix next

Tied to the focus areas you picked during onboarding, so the advice connects to what you actually want to improve.

AI-generated feedback can be wrong. It may miss faults, flag faults that didn't happen, or mis-estimate a speed. Treat it as a learning aid and always defer to your instructor, your supervising driver, the Highway Code and the law.

A short word on privacy

Mirror-check analysis happens on your phone, using Apple's on-device Vision framework to measure head movement. No facial images are ever uploaded or transmitted. Only derived results leave the device — whether a check was detected, head-turn angle summaries, timestamps and the turn's location.

Road-facing camera stills are only captured at detected events, never as continuous video. And if you'd rather no camera opened at all, telemetry-only mode gives you a full debrief without one.
